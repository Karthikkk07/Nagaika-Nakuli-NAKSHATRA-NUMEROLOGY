Nakshatra & Numerology

Important note. Numerology and Nakshatra study are traditional belief systems, not scientifically validated methods for predicting personality or events. There is no single universally accepted system that assigns each of the 27 Nakshatras to numbers 1–9, so this page keeps the two systems separate and lets you study them side by side.

Why Study the Nakshatras?

The subtle detail

The traditional almanac (Panchanga) has five limbs: Tithi, Vara, Nakshatra, Yoga and Karana. Some teachers link these to the five elements and see the Nakshatra, linked to air, as the most subtle and far-reaching of them. Where a sign gives the broad picture, the Nakshatra adds finer detail.

Stories, deities and symbols

Each Nakshatra has a presiding deity and a body of Vedic and Puranic stories and symbols. Many students use these to form a central theme for each Nakshatra, which is also an easy way to remember it.

Why the Moon’s Nakshatra

The Moon is traditionally the significator of the Nakshatras, which is why the Nakshatra the Moon occupies at birth is the one used on this page. The Moon’s point of exaltation, 3° of Taurus, falls in the second Pada of Krittika, and some schools treat Krittika as the start of the Nakshatra wheel.

The 27 Nakshatras at a Glance

In Vedic tradition the zodiac is divided into 27 Nakshatras (lunar mansions). Each spans 13°20′ and is divided into four Padas of 3°20′, giving 108 Padas in total. The ruling planets follow the commonly used Vimshottari Dasha sequence. Pada sounds are traditional naming sounds; spellings can vary by regional language and lineage.

The Four Padas

Every Nakshatra contains four equal Padas of 3°20′. The Pada is used in traditional chart interpretation and is also associated with a starting sound for naming in many regional traditions. Use the sounds above as a reference, then confirm the spelling with your family or regional tradition.

Simple Birth-Date Calculations

Birth number (day number): reduce the calendar day to one digit. Example: day 26 → 2 + 6 = 8.

Full-date total: add every digit in the date of birth and reduce to one digit, unless your chosen tradition preserves master numbers. Example: 14-08-1996 → 1+4+0+8+1+9+9+6 = 38 → 3+8 = 11 → 1+1 = 2.

Numerology calculation rules differ. Choose one method and apply it consistently; do not mix methods without saying so.

A Careful Way to Combine Nakshatra and Numerology

There is no universally agreed chart that maps Ashwini to one number, Bharani to another, and so on. A simple 1–9 repeating sequence across 27 Nakshatras is only a constructed exercise, not an established classical rule. For a more responsible study:

  • Write down the Moon Nakshatra, Pada and Nakshatra ruler for the birth.
  • Calculate the birth number and full-date total using the numerology tradition you have selected.
  • Study the symbolic meanings separately before making any comparison.
  • If a teacher or school gives a specific Nakshatra-to-number table, record its source and method.
